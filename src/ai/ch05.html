Part III · Building with models

Knowledge graphs and GraphRAG

A knowledge graph stores facts as typed relationships between entities, and GraphRAG retrieves by following those relationships; it earns its considerable cost only when the relationships themselves are the answer.

At a glance
  • Nodes are things, edges are typed relationships; a fact is subject, predicate, object.
  • Vector retrieval finds passages that look like the question; it struggles when the answer is spread across connected facts.
  • In GraphRAG, vectors find the entry points and the graph expands from them.
  • People design the schema from the questions it must answer; machines populate the edges, most trusted sources first.
  • The hard parts are entity resolution, provenance on every fact, and access control at every hop.

Constraints keep the graph honest: a unique stable id per node, and each edge type allowing only certain endpoint types (COVERS only from a runbook to a service). A service depending on itself is an error, while cycles between services can be real. The same shape fits other domains: a legal research product, for example, would have cases, statutes and topics as nodes, with CITES, OVERRULES, AMENDS and TAGGED_WITH as edges, and jurisdiction, court, date and status as properties.

Keeping it current

  • Incremental, coupled by identity. New facts are added without a rebuild, but a new document may show two nodes are one (merge) or one is two (split), which ripples through their edges.
  • Derived layers go stale: node embeddings, graph-algorithm results, and community summaries, which are often the costly part to recompute.
  • Changed extraction is a migration. A new prompt, model or schema leaves earlier facts inconsistent: re-extract, or record which version wrote each fact.
  • Deletion needs provenance. Removing a document's contribution is only possible if every fact records where it came from.
  • Access control at every hop. A traversal can reach nodes the asker may not see; the permission filter must hold on each step, the graph form of the tenant-filter trap in Chapter 4.
How to say it

"We'd start with ten to twenty competency questions and check that plain vector-plus-keyword retrieval can't answer them before building a graph."

"People own the schema and identity rules; systems of record fill most edges; model extraction is reviewed by sample, and every fact carries provenance so we can audit and delete it."

Questions

  1. When does GraphRAG beat plain vector retrieval, and when is it overkill?

    Answer

    When the answer lives in relationships or is spread over connected facts, such as the impact of a dependency failing. It is overkill when the questions are "find the passage that answers this", where vector plus keyword search is simpler and usually enough.

  2. What do people decide, and what do machines fill, in a knowledge graph?

    Answer

    People decide the schema from the questions, which source is trusted for which fact, the identity rules and the review. Machines fill edges from systems of record first, then curated sources, then reviewed model extraction.

  3. Why must every fact carry provenance?

    Answer

    To audit a wrong answer, to re-extract facts written by an older pipeline version, and to delete a document's contribution when the document is removed.
