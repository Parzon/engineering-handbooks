Production AI operations

Running an AI feature in production adds three things to ordinary operations: version everything that changes behavior, including the model and the prompt; gate every such change on evals; and expect incidents in which every request succeeds while the answers are wrong.

At a glance
  • Version the code, the exact model, the prompt, retrieval settings, the index and its embedding model, and the eval set; every answer traceable to all of them.
  • Never depend on a model alias such as "latest": a provider moving it changes your system without a deploy.
  • No prompt, model or retrieval change ships without the eval suite against the baseline; safety cases pass every run.
  • One rollback per kind of change, decided before shipping: code, prompt or model, feature, index, schema.
  • Quality indicators join the usual service levels: eval pass rate per release, refusal and fallback rates, grounded share of sampled answers, cost per request.

Service levels for an AI feature

An SLI is what is measured (the share of requests answered without error; the p95 time to first token); an SLO is the internal target over a window (99.5% over 30 days; that p95 under three seconds), whose gap to 100% is the error budget; an SLA is the contractual promise, set looser than the SLO. AI adds quality indicators: the eval pass rate per release, refusal and fallback rates, the grounded share of sampled answers, and the cost per request. The reference service's proposed objectives include answers completed (99% of model calls not canceled ending well) and answer quality (the gate passes on the production model each release, with safety cases at 100%), beside availability and latency (Handbook 2, Chapter 17).

Questions

  1. Why is a model alias such as "latest" dangerous in production?

    Answer

    The provider can move it to a different model at any time, changing your system's behavior without a deploy, a review or an eval run.

  2. What is the rollback for a prompt change, a new feature, a re-embedding and a schema change?

    Answer

    Prompt: switch the version back in configuration if prompts are configuration; if the prompt is code, as in the reference service, redeploy the previous tag. Feature: its flag (or, for the AI itself, the off switch). Re-embedding: switch back to the old index kept beside the new one; a re-embed in place, as in the reference service, has no switch back, only keyword search for the stale sections until it finishes. Schema: do not roll back; roll the code back alone and fix forward.

  3. What quality indicators would you add to the service levels of an AI feature?

    Answer

    The eval pass rate per release, refusal and fallback rates, the grounded share of sampled answers, invented citations, and the cost per request, beside availability and latency.
